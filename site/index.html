Five screens, one idea

Every number on every screen comes from the chain and carries its source. Nothing is decorative, nothing is invented to fill a gap.

Your card

Your name from the wallet, what you hold, how long you have held it, and your weight in the vault. The age is read from the oldest signature on your position account, not estimated.

Pulse

The whole vault at a glance: how much is staked, what flowed in and out over 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, and how the exit queue matures over the next two days.

Flow

The live floor. Every finalized stake, unstake and withdrawal as it lands, with the day's biggest move pinned above the stream and a gentle buzz when something arrives.

Queue

What is waiting to leave and when it frees. An unstake request is not money out — this screen keeps the difference visible.

Staking

Choose what goes into one transaction and how many transactions to send. One wallet approval covers up to sixteen, and a progress bar follows each one until the chain confirms it.

How it works

The short version: your keys stay in your phone, our server never touches them, and every claim on screen can be checked on chain.

your keys
The app cannot read your seed phrase or private key. Seed Vault holds them, your wallet approves every transaction, and the wallet — not this app — broadcasts it.
your data
No account, no sign-up, no analytics. The public address you connect is stored on the phone so you are not asked twice, and Disconnect erases it.
the numbers
An independent indexer reads the staking program directly and labels every value exact, estimated or unavailable, and every reading fresh, aging or stale.
the history
Coverage begins when the indexer began. Where history is incomplete the app says so, instead of showing a partial picture as a whole one.
alerts
Scheduled by the phone itself, using the exact on-chain unlock time. No push server, nothing about you leaves the device.

Questions

do I need SKR?
No. Pulse, Flow and Queue work with no wallet at all, and you can inspect any public address on the card screen. SKR is needed only to stake.
what does staking cost?
The network fee, a few thousand lamports per transaction. A wallet's first stake also pays rent for its position account, which stays with the account. The app takes nothing.
why several transactions?
Because each one is a separate on-chain stake. The app can send up to sixteen from a single approval, and each needs at least 1 SKR.
who made it?
Alex Kosa, an SKR staker who wanted this for himself. Source under MIT at github.com/busminer/skr-ecosystem-eyes. Questions: skr-eyes@alexkosa.dev.
