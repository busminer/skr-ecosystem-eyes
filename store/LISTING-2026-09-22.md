## Long description — exactly as it now sits in the portal draft

Rewritten from the text that was actually live, not from the 08-23 file: the
live copy was longer, better and already covered Top and the radar. Only the
staking went out of it — the opening clause "and lets you stake from the same
screen", the whole "Stake without leaving" section, and the tips line — and one
new paragraph, "Reading only", went in.

SKR Eyes shows the SKR staking vault as it actually is, second by second, and where you stand in it. Your wallet tells you what you hold. This tells you what the vault is doing.

The living vault. Every stake falls into the vault as a phone with the staker's .skr name under it; every exit hangs above it, cooling for its 48 hours, and drops when it is withdrawn. Larger stakes fall larger and slower. The pile under it all is the 4.95 billion SKR that stays. Nothing here is drawn from imagination: every phone is a finalized move on chain, and you can tap any of them for its receipt.

Where you stand. Everybody who stakes, in order: by stake, by what each position has earned, and by places moved since yesterday's scan. The fifty-one treasury-size wallets are set aside, so number one on the list is a person. Your own place rides at the top of it, and the radar puts all forty six thousand on one dial: six rings by tier, your phone gold among them, anybody tappable for their name.

Live from the vault. The feed of stakes, exits and withdrawals as they finalize, filtered by kind and size, with the day's biggest moves pinned on top. A large stake arrives on a rising note, a large exit on a low tone that bends down, a withdrawal on a short fall, on any screen, each with a short buzz. Or switch them off.

Your staker card. Your .skr name, your days in stake, your weight in the vault, what your position has earned and where you stand among people, on a card you can share as a picture. Three switches decide what leaves the phone: hide your name, hide your amount, or keep your place off the card. With the name hidden the card shows your tier only, never the exact place.

The exit queue. What is cooling down, what is ready, and when each position matures. Your phone can wake you at the exact on-chain unlock time; the alert is set on the device itself, and no address is sent anywhere for it.

Reading only. SKR Eyes does not stake, unstake or withdraw, and it builds no transaction of any kind. Connecting a wallet only tells the app which address to show, and you can paste any public address instead.

Eleven languages: English, Deutsch, 中文, Русский, Español, Português, Türkçe, Tiếng Việt, Indonesia, 한국어, 日本語. The app follows your phone's language and can be switched by hand on the Alerts screen.

Free and open source, built by one Seeker. Tips go straight to kosa.skr. Built on our own indexer, read at finalized commitment. Every number carries its source, and estimates are labelled as estimates. SKR Eyes never asks for a seed phrase, never holds keys and never takes custody of funds.

SKR Eyes is an independent product. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Solana Foundation or Solana Mobile.

## The three large-move sounds were replaced, 22.09

The old set was literal — an old phone ringing for a stake, a cinematic tudum
for an exit, a door closing for a withdrawal. Alex heard them on the phone and
asked for something neutral. The new three are instruments: a fifth going up
for a stake arriving, one low tone bending down for an exit asked for, a short
dry fall for a withdrawal that actually left. Written by synthesis like the
rest of the set (`brand/make-move-sounds.py`), picked by ear on the Seeker.

The sentence describing them changed in the long description, on the site, and
on the Alerts screen inside the app — the last one in eleven languages.

## Testing instructions for the reviewer

**The whole app works without holding any SKR and without a wallet.** Nothing
in it signs, and there is no transaction to test.

1. **Vault**, **Flow** and **Top** need no wallet at all. Open the app and they
   fill on their own.
2. Open **Me** and paste any public address that holds a SKR position, for
   example `BmJaEmaEURBDpj91GUTvJcpKy2MAu8ZtRFRPaMpEuuax` — the developer's own
   wallet, offered so no third party's position is used as a fixture. The card
   fills with that address's position, age, vault weight and place. No
   signature is requested and no wallet connection is needed.
3. **Connect Solana Mobile** on the same screen does exactly what pasting does,
   only it reads the address from the wallet instead of the clipboard. The
   authorization is read-only — the app calls `authorize` / `reauthorize` with
   an empty feature set and never asks the wallet to sign anything. Disconnect
   erases the address, the wallet's label and the stored authorization token.
4. **Alerts** asks for the notification permission and can send a test
   notification immediately.
5. **The example address has no unstake running.** Its cooldown countdown is
   therefore not displayed — there is nothing pending to count down to. The
   maturing of the exit queue can be seen at the foot of **Vault**, which needs
   no wallet.
6. **On `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`:** an unlock is a single on-chain timestamp, and
   the whole promise of the Alerts screen is that the phone wakes at that
   moment rather than "some time that hour". The alert is scheduled locally,
   there is no push server, and the permission stays revocable by the user.
   `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is deliberately not requested.
7. **The tip button on Alerts** opens a Solana Pay link, or a QR code for
   another phone. The app fills in recipient, amount and token and hands the
   link to whatever wallet is installed; the wallet builds, signs and sends it.
   SKR Eyes neither builds nor sends it, and the tip is entirely optional.
8. **If you unpack the APK:** the two matches for `signAndSendTransactions` in
   the JS bundle are string tables inside `@solana/web3.js` and the Mobile
   Wallet Adapter protocol library. The app calls neither.
